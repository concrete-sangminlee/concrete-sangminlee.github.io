# Teaching Statement

Sang Min Lee · Ph.D. Candidate in Artificial Intelligence, Seoul National University

My students have mostly been architecture and engineering majors meeting either structural mechanics or computing for the first time, and often both at once. I have served as a teaching assistant for eight courses at Seoul National University since 2021, from mechanics of materials and prestressed concrete to Arduino labs and deep neural networks, and was head TA for *Introduction to IoT, AI, and Big Data* in Fall 2022. That semester the College of Engineering gave me its Outstanding Teaching Assistant Award. Before graduate school, I spent a year and a half teaching mathematics to students from low-income families with Edushare Korea, as a teacher and then lead teacher of its Uiwang Bugok center. These experiences shaped three principles I teach by.

## Students learn by building and measuring

Equations become meaningful when students see the behavior they describe. In *Structural Experiment and Material*, I ran experiments on Euler buckling, truss deflection, and seismic design on a two-way shake table, where students compared measured response with what they had calculated. In three IoT courses, I ran labs on Arduino UNO, ESP32, Teensy, and STM boards and evaluated student projects, so that sensing and control were things students made work rather than read about. I bring the same approach to machine learning: in *Architecture and AI*, taught each spring from 2022 to 2024, I led hands-on sessions in which students trained deep neural networks themselves before discussing why they behave as they do.

## Lower the barrier, keep the standard

Many engineering students arrive believing that programming or machine learning is not for them. In *Computation in Architectural Engineering*, I taught MATLAB labs for structural engineering students, and in *Mechanics of Materials for Architectural Engineering*, I led problem sessions on structural fundamentals. In both, I break problems into steps small enough to start on, while asking students to justify each step, not just reach the answer. Teaching mathematics at Edushare Korea taught me that a student who has fallen behind needs a first success quickly and a clear path from there, and that low expectations do them no favors.

## Engineering judgment is part of the subject

When machine learning enters structural engineering, students must learn not only how to fit a model but when to distrust it. My research is about exactly this: models that work with little failure data, few sensors, and safety-critical codes. In teaching, I ask students to state what their model has never seen, how it would fail, and what an engineer should check before acting on its output. In *Prestressed Concrete*, where I managed the course with a focus on post-tensioned structures, I saw how much design depends on understanding assumptions and limits; I want students to treat data-driven tools with the same care.

## Courses I can teach

I am prepared to teach undergraduate and graduate courses in:

- machine learning for civil and structural engineering, with labs on inspection, monitoring, and wind data;
- structural health monitoring and non-destructive testing;
- data-driven wind engineering;
- programming, sensing, and IoT for engineers;
- foundational courses in structural mechanics and concrete structures.

I would also like to develop a project course in which teams build and evaluate an end-to-end inspection or monitoring system, from sensor to model to an engineering decision, drawing on my work on edge-AI impact-echo testing.

## Mentoring

In our lab I have worked closely with fellow graduate students on papers they led, on deep SVDD for monitoring and on impact-echo testing behind containment liner plates. As a mentor, I want every student I mentor to leave able to frame a question, design an experiment that could prove them wrong, and write it up clearly. I will measure my teaching by whether students can do those things on their own.
